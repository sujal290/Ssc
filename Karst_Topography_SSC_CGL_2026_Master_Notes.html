01 • Core concept

How does Karst Topography form?

Rainwater absorbs carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and soil, forming weak carbonic acid. This water enters joints, cracks and bedding planes in soluble limestone. Over time, dissolution enlarges these pathways and produces a distinctive surface and underground drainage system.

Rainwater + CO₂ → weak carbonic acid → limestone dissolution → underground drainage → karst features

02 • Surface landforms

Karst Surface Features

🕳️

Doline

A closed depression or sinkhole produced mainly by solution and/or collapse. Think: basic karst sinkhole.
〰️

Uvala

A larger, irregular depression commonly associated with the enlargement/coalescence of dolines.
🏞️

Polje

A very large, enclosed karst depression, commonly with a relatively flat floor and intermittent drainage.
⬇️

Ponor

An opening through which surface water disappears into the underground karst drainage system; also called a swallow hole in many texts.
🪨

Limestone Pavement

Exposed limestone surface broken into blocks by joints, with solution grooves and channels.
⛰️

Karst Tower / Cone

Residual limestone hills produced by prolonged dissolution and denudation; towers are steep-sided, cones are more conical.

04 • Never confuse

Stalactite vs Stalagmite

STALACTITE ↓

Attached to the ceiling. Mineral-rich water drips downward and deposits material.

TITE → ceiling / DOWN

STALAGMITE ↑

Builds upward from the floor where dripping water deposits material.

MITE → floor / UP
Best memory: “Stalactite holds tight to the ceiling.” The mnemonic is only for recall; the scientific distinction is the location and direction of growth.
05 • SSC CGL traps

Common Option-Traps

Trap 1 — Stalagmite hanging from the ceiling

Wrong. A stalagmite grows upward from the floor. Ceiling → stalactite.

Trap 2 — Stalactite rising from the floor

Wrong. Floor → stalagmite.

Trap 3 — Cave pillar formed solely by wind abrasion

Wrong. Karst cave deposits are associated with groundwater, dissolution and mineral precipitation. Wind abrasion is not the defining process here.

Trap 4 — Karst is mainly a volcanic landform

Wrong. Karst is classically associated with soluble rocks, especially limestone, and groundwater action.

Trap 5 — Ponor is a cave deposit

Wrong. A ponor is an opening where surface water enters the underground karst drainage system.
