1. Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY)

Paper focus: eligibility/entry age.

Core concept

PMSBY is a government-backed accident insurance scheme associated with the financial-inclusion/social-security framework. The paper tests the entry-age bracket, but SSC can easily ask the premium, nature of cover, renewal pattern, or difference from other Jan Suraksha schemes.

FeatureExam note
NatureAccident insurance cover; not a life-investment product.
Entry age18–70 years for enrolment, as reflected in the supplied paper.
RenewalAnnual cover, subject to the scheme rules and payment of premium.
Important distinctionPMSBY = accident insurance; PMJJBY = life insurance.

Do not confuse

  • PMSBY → accident insurance.
  • PMJJBY → life insurance.
  • APY → pension scheme.
SSC trap: changing “accident insurance” into “life insurance”, or swapping PMSBY and PMJJBY age/benefit details.

2. Mathura School of Art

Paper focus: indigenous character and Yaksha influence.

Core concept

Mathura developed a major indigenous sculptural tradition in northern India. Its early sculptural vocabulary is strongly connected with local traditions, including Yaksha imagery. It became an important centre for Buddhist, Jain and Brahmanical sculpture.

MathuraGandhara
Indian/local artistic baseStrong Hellenistic-Greco-Roman influence
Red/pinkish spotted sandstone is characteristicGrey schist is commonly associated
Emphasis on spiritual/inner expression and idealised formMore naturalistic modelling and detailed drapery
Yaksha tradition is an important backgroundClassical western artistic vocabulary is prominent

Beyond the question

  • Both schools contributed to the development of anthropomorphic Buddha images.
  • Mathura also produced Jain Tirthankara and Brahmanical images.
  • Mathura art flourished especially under Kushana patronage.
Memory hook: Gandhara = Grey + Greek influence; Mathura = Red sandstone + indigenous Indian base.

3. Gram-positive vs Gram-negative bacteria

Paper focus: cell-wall structure and Gram staining.

FeatureGram-positiveGram-negative
PeptidoglycanThickThin
Outer membraneAbsentPresent
LPSAbsentPresent in outer membrane
Teichoic acidsCharacteristicAbsent
Typical Gram-stain colourPurple/violetPink/red

Gram-staining sequence

  1. Crystal violet — primary stain.
  2. Iodine — mordant.
  3. Alcohol/acetone — decolouriser.
  4. Safranin — counterstain.
Classic trap: Gram-negative bacteria do not have a thicker peptidoglycan layer. Their peptidoglycan is thinner, but they have an additional outer membrane.

4. Major Industrial Regions of India

Paper focus: identification of a non-listed industrial region.

Core map set

The commonly taught major industrial-region grouping includes areas such as the Mumbai–Pune belt, Gujarat/Ahmedabad–Vadodara belt, Chota Nagpur region, Hugli belt, Gurugram–Delhi–Meerut belt, Visakhapatnam–Guntur belt, Bengaluru–Tamil Nadu belt, and Kollam–Thiruvananthapuram belt.

What to connect with each belt

  • Chota Nagpur: mineral resources, coal, iron ore, heavy industry.
  • Mumbai–Pune: engineering, automobiles, chemicals, petrochemicals and diversified manufacturing.
  • Hugli: jute, engineering and port-linked industries.
  • Gujarat: petrochemicals, textiles, chemicals and engineering.
  • Bengaluru–Tamil Nadu: automobiles, engineering, electronics/IT-linked industrial ecosystem.
Map strategy: learn the region as location + raw material + transport + flagship industries, not merely as a name list.

5. Bhakti Movement — Nirguna Tradition

Paper focus: Kabir as a Nirguna Bhakti saint.

Nirguna vs Saguna

NirgunaSaguna
Ultimate reality without a fixed physical formGod approached through a manifest form/attribute
Kabir is a major representativeSurdas, Mirabai and Chaitanya are associated with devotional traditions centred on manifest forms
Strong emphasis on inner devotion and critique of empty ritualismOften expressed through love, image/form, stories and personal devotion

Kabir — exam zone

  • Associated with the Nirguna stream.
  • Critiqued rigid ritualism and social/religious divisions.
  • His verses are preserved through oral and literary traditions, including the Bijak tradition.
  • Influence is visible in the broader North Indian devotional milieu.
Trap: do not classify every Bhakti saint under one single ideological box; SSC often tests the broad stream and a distinctive association.

6. Census 2011 — Population Growth

Paper focus: decadal growth during 2001–2011.

Concept

Decadal growth rate compares population change over a ten-year census interval. For SSC, distinguish absolute growth, percentage growth, population density, and population share.

Paper answer: Meghalaya is the intended option among the choices shown in the supplied paper.

Important Census traps

  • Highest population ≠ highest growth rate.
  • High density ≠ high growth.
  • Growth can be affected by both natural increase and migration.
  • Census 2011 is the latest completed Indian population census traditionally used in many SSC static-GK questions; do not mix it with later projections.

7. Actual Population Growth Formula

Paper focus: natural increase plus net migration.

Formula: Actual growth = (Births − Deaths) + (In-migration − Out-migration).

Four building blocks

  • Births − deaths = natural increase.
  • In-migration − out-migration = net migration.
  • Actual population change combines both.
TermMeaning
Natural increaseBirths minus deaths
Net migrationImmigration/in-migration minus emigration/out-migration
Positive growthOverall addition exceeds losses
Negative growthOverall losses exceed additions

8. DNA Replication — Enzymes

Paper focus: DNA ligase as the “molecular glue”.

EnzymeMain job
HelicaseUnwinds DNA by disrupting hydrogen bonding between complementary bases.
Topoisomerase / DNA gyraseRelieves torsional strain and supercoiling.
PrimaseProduces the RNA primer needed to start synthesis.
DNA polymeraseExtends the new DNA strand and, depending on the polymerase, performs proofreading/other processing.
DNA ligaseSeals nicks and joins DNA fragments through phosphodiester-bond formation.

Okazaki fragments

Because DNA polymerase synthesizes DNA in the 5′→3′ direction, discontinuous synthesis occurs on the lagging strand. The resulting fragments are joined by DNA ligase.

One-line memory: Helicase opens → Primase starts → Polymerase builds → Ligase joins.

9. Heat Transfer & Cooking-Utensil Handles

Paper focus: poor thermal conductors.

Why wood/Bakelite?

They have relatively low thermal conductivity, so heat travels through the handle much less readily than through metals. This reduces the rate at which the gripping surface becomes dangerously hot.

ModeMeaning
ConductionTransfer through direct molecular interaction; especially important in solids.
ConvectionHeat transfer through bulk movement of fluids.
RadiationEnergy transfer by electromagnetic radiation; no material medium is required.
Metal pan body → good heat conduction. Insulating handle → low conduction. SSC often tests this contrast.

10. Uniform Civil Code (UCC)

Paper focus: personal/civil matters such as marriage, divorce, maintenance and inheritance.

Constitutional location

Article 44 of the Constitution directs the State to endeavour to secure a uniform civil code for citizens throughout India. It is located in the Directive Principles of State Policy.

Topic boundary

  • Marriage and divorce
  • Maintenance
  • Inheritance/succession
  • Other personal-law/civil-law matters depending on the legal framework
Do not mix: UCC is about a common civil framework; it is not itself a criminal code, tax code, election code or trade regulation.

11. National Single Window System (NSWS)

Paper focus: purpose and institutional association.

What it does

NSWS is designed as a digital facilitation platform through which investors/businesses can identify and access approvals and clearances relevant to establishing or operating businesses in India.

Exam distinctions

  • Think business approvals / investor facilitation.
  • It is associated with the Ministry of Commerce & Industry, not the Ministry of Finance.
  • It is a facilitation mechanism, not a replacement for every substantive approval authority.
Assertion–Reason logic: the purpose statement can be correct even when the ministry named in the reason is wrong.

12. Global Peace Index (GPI)

Paper focus: the least-peaceful country in the 2024 edition.

The supplied paper identifies Yemen as the intended answer for GPI 2024.

How to study an index

Do not memorise only one rank. Learn the issuing body and broad dimensions. The Global Peace Index is produced by the Institute for Economics & Peace (IEP) and assesses peace through a basket of indicators relating to societal safety/security, ongoing conflict and militarisation.

SSC trap set

  • GPI ≠ Global Terrorism Index.
  • GPI ≠ Human Development Index.
  • IEP is the relevant organisation for GPI.
  • Rankings are edition-specific; never treat a 2024 ranking as automatically the 2026 ranking.

13. India's NDC & Climate Commitments

Paper focus: emissions-intensity reduction target for 2030.

Paper fact: India's updated NDC target in the supplied paper is a 45% reduction in emissions intensity of GDP by 2030 from the 2005 level.

Understand the wording

Emissions intensity is not the same as total emissions. It relates emissions to an economic output measure such as GDP. Therefore, a reduction in emissions intensity does not necessarily mean absolute emissions fall by the same percentage.

Neighbour concepts

  • NDC: nationally determined contribution under the Paris Agreement framework.
  • UNFCCC: United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change.
  • Net zero: a balance between emissions released and removals over the relevant accounting framework.
  • Renewable energy: solar, wind, hydro, biomass and other renewable sources.

14. Indian Textile Industry

Paper focus: employment significance.

Why textiles matter

  • Large labour-intensive sector.
  • Strong links with agriculture through cotton, jute, silk and other fibres.
  • Includes spinning, weaving/knitting, processing, garmenting and technical textiles.
  • Important for exports and regional industrialisation.

Major fibre chains

FibreTypical association
CottonWestern and central India; major textile centres across several states.
JuteStrongly associated with the lower Ganga/Hugli industrial belt.
SilkMultiple producing regions; sericulture-based clusters.
WoolRaw-material and processing centres in different northern/western regions.
Trap: textile industry is not exclusively synthetic and is not entirely public-sector owned.

15. Atmosphere — Layers & Ionosphere

Paper focus: meteors and the ionosphere.

LayerKey exam feature
TroposphereMost weather phenomena; temperature generally decreases with altitude.
StratosphereContains the ozone-rich region; relatively stable and important for aviation.
MesosphereMeteors commonly burn up here.
ThermosphereVery high temperatures; ionised particles; auroral phenomena occur in this broad region.
ExosphereExtremely thin outer region, gradually merging into space.

Ionosphere

The ionosphere is an ionised region extending through parts of the upper atmosphere, mainly associated with the thermosphere and overlapping other atmospheric layers. It is important for radio-wave propagation and space-weather effects.

Trap: “ionosphere is inside the troposphere” is incorrect.

17. Thrissur Pooram

Paper focus: Kudamattam.

Core facts

  • Major festival of Kerala, centred on Thrissur.
  • Held at the Vadakkunnathan Temple and associated with participating temple groups.
  • Famous for decorated/caparisoned elephants, percussion ensembles and spectacular processions.
  • Kudamattam: colourful ceremonial umbrella exchange/display performed atop decorated elephants.

Do not mix with

  • Vallam Kali: traditional boat races, especially associated with Kerala's backwaters and Onam-season festivities.
  • Sabarimala: pilgrimage centre associated with Lord Ayyappa, not the venue that defines Thrissur Pooram.

18. Carnatic Music — Kriti

Paper focus: Pallavi, Anupallavi and Charanam.

Kriti

A kriti is a central compositional form in Carnatic classical music. The commonly taught structure includes Pallavi, Anupallavi and Charanam.

TermRole
PallaviOpening thematic section.
AnupallaviSecond section developing the musical idea.
CharanamConcluding/main extended section.

Major Trinity

SSC frequently links the Carnatic Trinity with Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar and Syama Sastri.

Trap: Thumri, Khayal and Dhrupad are primarily associated with Hindustani classical music, while kriti is strongly associated with Carnatic music.

19. International Solar Alliance (ISA)

Paper focus: origin and membership rule.

Origin

ISA was launched by India and France in connection with the COP21 process in Paris in 2015. It aims to promote solar-energy deployment and cooperation.

Membership trap

The original framing focused on countries located fully or partly between the Tropics, but the membership framework was later broadened. Therefore, the statement that membership is strictly restricted only to countries entirely between the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn is too restrictive.

Exam links

  • Solar cooperation
  • International organisation headquartered in India
  • Climate diplomacy
  • Renewable-energy financing, technology and deployment

20. Khushwant Singh — Train to Pakistan

Paper focus: literary treatment of Partition.

Core

Train to Pakistan is a novel set against the backdrop of the 1947 Partition. It portrays the human cost of communal violence and the breakdown—and attempted recovery—of social relationships in a borderland community.

Why the Reason is wrong in the paper

It is not a non-fiction diplomatic biography documenting negotiations between British officials and Indian political leaders. The question deliberately changes the genre and subject.

SSC genre trap: novel ≠ biography ≠ autobiography ≠ historical document.

21. Karst Topography & Cave Features

Paper focus: stalactite vs stalagmite.

FeatureWhere / how
StalactiteHangs from the ceiling.
StalagmiteGrows upward from the floor.
Pillar/columnForms when a stalactite and stalagmite join.
FlowstoneSheet-like mineral deposit formed by flowing water.

Surface karst terms

  • Sinkhole/doline: enclosed depression.
  • Uvala: larger irregular depression formed by coalescence/enlargement of depressions.
  • Polje: large, often flat-floored karst depression.
  • Karst tower: residual limestone tower/steep-sided relief in tropical karst settings.
Memory: STALACtite = STALACtites cling to the ceiling. Stalagmite rises from the ground.

22. Global Hunger Index (GHI)

Paper focus: four component indicators.

Four components

  1. Undernourishment
  2. Child wasting
  3. Child stunting
  4. Child mortality

Understand the child indicators

  • Wasting: low weight-for-height; commonly associated with acute undernutrition.
  • Stunting: low height-for-age; commonly associated with chronic undernutrition.
  • Child mortality: reflects severe deprivation and associated health/nutrition conditions.

Score structure

The GHI uses a 100-point scale, with higher values representing a worse hunger situation. The supplied paper's infographic also shows severity bands; learn the concept of the scale rather than relying only on one year's country score.

Trap: GHI is not simply a “food production” or “per-capita income” index.

23. INSAT-3DS

Paper focus: meteorological mission.

Primary purpose: improve meteorological observations, weather forecasting and disaster-warning capabilities.

What to connect

  • INSAT meteorological satellites support weather observation and forecasting.
  • Weather satellites can provide information on cloud systems, atmospheric parameters and storm development.
  • Such data are important for cyclone monitoring, rainfall assessment and disaster preparedness.

Trap options

  • NavIC positioning → navigation, not INSAT-3DS's primary mission.
  • Celestial X-ray polarisation → astronomy/science mission category.
  • Military broadband → different communications/security application.

24. Migration & Age-Sex Composition

Paper focus: selective migration and demographic structure.

Why migration changes composition

Migration is often selective by age, sex, education, occupation and economic status. Working-age adults can be especially mobile. Therefore migration can change the age-sex pyramid in both the source and destination areas.

AreaPossible demographic effect
Source/originLoss of working-age population; changes in sex ratio may occur depending on who migrates.
DestinationIncrease in working-age population and potentially stronger labour-force concentration.

Push–pull framework

  • Push: unemployment, low wages, insecurity, environmental stress, lack of opportunities.
  • Pull: jobs, education, higher wages, services, safety, urban opportunities.
Assertion–Reason: the reason explains the assertion because selective migration directly alters the demographic composition of both regions.

25. Lysosome

Paper focus: intracellular digestion and cellular waste breakdown.

Core function

Lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes that digest macromolecules and help break down cellular waste and worn-out components. They are especially important in intracellular digestion and cellular recycling.

OrganelleHigh-yield function
LysosomeIntracellular digestion, degradation and recycling.
RibosomeProtein synthesis.
CentrosomeMicrotubule organisation; important in animal-cell division.
ChloroplastPhotosynthesis in plants/algae.
MitochondrionMajor site of aerobic cellular respiration/ATP production.
Memory: Lysosome = lytic enzymes = breakdown.
